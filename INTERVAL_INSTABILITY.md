# INTERVAL_INSTABILITY.md — class size vs. gate8-rejected intervals, by feature-vector dimension

SIMULATED: this raises, and does not fix, the underlying model question —
per Standing Rule 10, this is a proposal, not an amendment; `PROTOCOL.md`
is not modified. Full data: `SIMULATED_INTERVAL_INSTABILITY_SWEEP.tsv`
(72 rows, generated by `interval_instability_sweep.py`, a new file — read-
only imports of `stake_ablation.py`'s estimator and
`gate8_interval_informativeness.py`'s own informativeness check; neither
of those files is modified).

## Method

For each of 2 arms (CORE_HR, DDR_SIGNALING) × 3 feature-vector dimensions
× 12 class sizes (n=12 to n=160 per class), 15 independent random
subsamples were drawn from the existing synthetic population (without
replacement, same methodology as `stake_ablation.py`'s realistic-n
scenarios), PROTOCOL.md §7.1's estimator was re-fit and bootstrapped
(B=300 per trial, reduced from PROTOCOL's B=2000 for tractability across
~1000 trials — disclosed, not silent), and each trial's resulting interval
was passed through `gate8_interval_informativeness.py`'s own,
already-committed informativeness check. The three dimensions:

| dimension | feature subset | why chosen |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | `{SBS3}` alone | isolates the specific feature at issue in P-DEC-1, KDE-estimated |
| 2 | `{LOH, SBS3}` | adds one categorical (Jeffreys-proportion, not KDE) feature |
| 3 | `{LOH, GIS, SBS3}` | the full feature vector PROTOCOL.md §7.1 specifies |

## Headline finding 1 — there is no single clean "threshold n"; the failure rate is non-monotonic in n

For CORE_HR/`{SBS3}` alone, the UNINFORMATIVE fraction across 15 trials is:

| n | 12 | 15 | 20 | 25 | 30 | 40 | 50 | 65 | 75 | 100 | 130 | 160 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| UNINFORMATIVE/15 | 9 | 12 | 13 | **15** | **15** | **15** | **15** | 13 | 11 | 6 | 3 | 5 |

**The failure rate is WORST (100%) in the middle of this range (n=25–50), not at the smallest n tested.** At the very smallest n (12), a distinct and more severe failure mode dominates instead (see Headline finding 2), which is not counted as `UNINFORMATIVE` in this table — once n is large enough that this more severe mode stops occurring but still too small for the bootstrap CI to actually stabilize, every single trial fails gate8. Only past n≈65–100 does the failure rate begin a real, if noisy, decline. **A report that named a single "instability threshold" would misstate this shape.**

## Headline finding 2 — below "wide," there is a worse failure mode: the estimate can be undefined

At the smallest n tested (12, sometimes 15/20), some trials do not merely
produce a wide interval — the class-conditional Gaussian-kernel density
at `EVAL_POINT` underflows to exactly `0.0` for one class in floating
point (no resampled point close enough, relative to the tiny Silverman
bandwidth at that n, to leave a nonzero kernel sum), making the likelihood
ratio a `0/x` or division-by-zero — **not computable at all**, not merely
imprecise:

| arm | dimension | n=12 | n=15 | n=20 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| CORE_HR | 1 ({SBS3}) | 5/15 | 2/15 | 2/15 |
| CORE_HR | 2 ({LOH,SBS3}) | 5/15 | 3/15 | 0/15 |
| CORE_HR | 3 (full) | 5/15 | 0/15 | 0/15 |
| DDR_SIGNALING | 1 | 1/15 | 0/15 | 1/15 |
| DDR_SIGNALING | 2 | 0/15 | 0/15 | 1/15 |
| DDR_SIGNALING | 3 | 1/15 | 0/15 | 0/15 |

This does not appear at all past n=20 in this sweep. It is reported here
as its own category (`ZERO_DENSITY_UNDEFINED`, distinct from and more
severe than gate8's `UNINFORMATIVE`) because gate8 itself cannot catch it
— gate8 evaluates a table row that already contains numbers; an estimator
that sometimes cannot produce a number at all is a failure mode upstream
of anything gate8 checks.

## Headline finding 3 — DDR_SIGNALING never reliably stabilizes within the tested range for SBS3-involving subsets

| arm | dimension | n=160 UNINFORMATIVE/15 |
|---|---|---|
| CORE_HR | 1 ({SBS3}) | 5/15 (33%) |
| CORE_HR | 2 ({LOH,SBS3}) | 0/15 |
| CORE_HR | 3 (full) | 0/15 |
| DDR_SIGNALING | 1 ({SBS3}) | **7/15 (47%)** |
| DDR_SIGNALING | 2 ({LOH,SBS3}) | 1/15 |
| DDR_SIGNALING | 3 (full) | 0/15 |

**DDR_SIGNALING's SBS3-alone marginal LR is still UNINFORMATIVE in nearly
half of independent trials even at n=160/class** — a class size already
well above the ~25-35 realistic Track B figure this project's own
`SIMULATION_SPEC.md` §6 derives for this gene group, and far above the
low-tens PALB2/RAD51C/RAD51D figures this task's own text names. This is
consistent with, and is the same underlying mechanism as,
`STAKE_ANALYSIS.md`'s finding that DDR_SIGNALING's SBS3 marginal sits
closer to the zero-clip boundary (Benign mean 0.08, vs. CORE_HR's 0.03 —
CORE_HR is actually numerically closer in absolute terms but has a
narrower spread relative to `EVAL_POINT`; see `FIDELITY_AUDIT.md`'s exact
figures) — the mechanism is boundary proximity relative to bandwidth at
small n, not gene group identity per se, but DDR_SIGNALING is the group
where it bites hardest in this simulator's specific parameterization.

## Headline finding 4 — dimension is confounded with WHICH feature is added, not a clean "more dimensions = worse" trend

This task's own framing hypothesized instability scales with feature-
vector dimension ("Multivariate KDE at n in the low tens is where that
estimator degrades... characterize the instability... by feature-vector
dimension"). **The data does not show a monotonic dimension effect** — at
matched n, adding features to the vector often makes the COMPOSITE
estimate LESS likely to be flagged `UNINFORMATIVE`, not more (compare
dimension 1 vs. 3 in Headline finding 3's table: CORE_HR goes from 5/15 to
0/15; DDR_SIGNALING from 7/15 to 0/15, at the SAME n=160). **The mechanism
is not a dimensionality effect in the classic multivariate-KDE curse-of-
dimensionality sense** — PROTOCOL.md §7.1's joint estimator is not a true
multivariate (joint) KDE at all; it is a PRODUCT of independently-fit 1-D
KDE/Jeffreys-proportion ratios (the conditional-independence factorization
§7.1 itself describes), so each feature's density is estimated in its own
1-D space, not in a shared d-dimensional volume that thins out as d grows.
**What actually happens**: PROTOCOL.md's LOH feature (Jeffreys `+0.5`-
corrected empirical proportion) is bounded and numerically stable by
construction — it cannot produce a `0/x` or an extreme outlier ratio the
way a small-sample Gaussian-kernel density evaluated near a sparse tail
can. GIS is a smoothly-distributed, non-clipped Gaussian marginal with no
boundary-proximity problem (`FIDELITY_AUDIT.md`). Multiplying SBS3's own
unstable ratio by these two well-behaved factors does not fix SBS3's own
instability, but it can pull the COMPOSITE point estimate and bootstrap
distribution away from the specific numeric extremes gate8's thresholds
flag, at the SAME nominal sample size. **The real driver in this data is
which specific feature (SBS3, and specifically its proximity to the [0,
0.95] clip boundary in the Benign class) is in the vector, far more than
how many features are in it.** This is a correction to the task's own
working hypothesis, reported per Standing Rule 2/4 rather than silently
matched to the expected shape.

## What this means for the model-choice question (raised, not fixed)

`PROTOCOL.md` §7.1 specifies this KDE/Jeffreys/product-of-marginals
estimator in place of penalized logistic regression. This sweep shows that
estimator, run exactly as specified, produces `gate8`-rejected (or
outright undefined) intervals across a wide, non-trivial band of class
sizes overlapping the realistic Track B range this project has already
cited (`SIMULATION_SPEC.md` §6) — not merely at the extreme low end, and
not resolved by "just use the full 3-feature vector" (dimension 3 is the
LEAST bad of the three tested, but CORE_HR/dimension-1 and
DDR_SIGNALING/dimension-1 are both still measurably unstable at n=160, and
DDR_SIGNALING's single-feature SBS3 marginal — which is exactly the
quantity `SIMULATED_RECOVERY_TABLE.tsv` reports as its own row — never
drops below 33% UNINFORMATIVE anywhere in this sweep). **This is evidence
bearing on the model choice, logged in `PROPOSED_DEVIATIONS.md`** — a
future task may weigh it against penalized logistic regression or a
regularized/shrinkage density estimator; this document and that entry do
not decide between them. `PROTOCOL.md` is not modified.
